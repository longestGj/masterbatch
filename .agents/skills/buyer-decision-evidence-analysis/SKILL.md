# Buyer decision evidence analysis

Study the buying decision relevant to the caller's question, not a generic persona. The caller provides the known business context, buyer or decision participant if known, offer/category, market/channel if relevant, existing evidence, decision question and stopping condition. Mark missing context as unknown; do not fill it with an industry-average buyer.

## Focus the inquiry

Start from current customers and transactions when W1 or the owner knows them. Identify what downstream decision the answer could change. Select only the applicable questions: who initiates, uses, evaluates, approves, pays or can block; the job and search or switching trigger; comparison and rejection criteria; proof needed to reduce risk; meaningful alternatives, including the current or internal solution; evaluation or trial steps and commercial terms before first purchase; and repeat or retention drivers. Buyer, market and channel differences matter only where they could change the conclusion. Adjacent segments may be proposed as hypotheses, never substituted for known customers.

## Build evidence proportional to the claim

Prefer supplied buyer discussions, owner or sales records and research within their actual scope. Add relevant association or procurement guidance, credible surveys, review or community evidence, professional sources, competitor material and search observations as the question warrants. Record source, date, sample or context, and what each source can establish. A competitor page shows its message, not the buyer's priority; an anecdote or forum post shows one experience, not market prevalence. Do not invent interviews, roles, conversion rates or consensus. When an external pattern conflicts with current business facts, present both, a plausible explanation and what would resolve it.

Check alternative explanations and meaningful counterexamples before treating a decision factor as general; if evidence is unavailable, say so. Stop when the decision has sufficient support and more research is unlikely to change it. If the deciding evidence is unavailable publicly, name the owner or internal data needed and leave the claim open.

Return concise prose or a small table covering the buyer/decision context, material observations, likely factors and objections, alternatives, relevant differences, limits and questions raised for W2 or W3. Distinguish observation from inference where material. Do not select the target customer, positioning, page strategy or marketing priority; the caller integrates the findings. No fixed JSON, persona database or separate report is required.
