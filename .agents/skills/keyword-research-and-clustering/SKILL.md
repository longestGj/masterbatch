# Keyword research and clustering

Find the language people may use for an offer, problem, comparison, selection, or question, then group it into query families useful to the calling Agent. This is research evidence, not a keyword-to-page map. The caller supplies the business and approved audience context, offer or topic, market and language where relevant, known pages and query evidence if available, the architecture question, and a stopping condition. Reuse adequate existing research before gathering more.

Discover candidate queries proportionally from supplied customer language, site and search data, relevant search tools and results, and visible alternative terminology. Include natural-language and low-volume questions when they clarify buyer tasks, entity relationships, or topic gaps; do not let a standard category list dictate the research. Keep observed queries distinct from generated hypotheses. For consequential observations, record source or tool, date, market or language, and scope. Metrics such as volume, difficulty, clicks, or rank are optional observations, never proof of buyer value or permission to create a page. Do not invent missing metrics or infer zero demand from missing data.

Cluster by shared intent, user task, topic, entity, decision question, and likely canonical answer responsibility. Distinguish synonyms and location variants from genuinely different information needs; note where existing pages overlap or compete for the same responsibility. Use representative search results when they materially help grouping, without claiming that a small sample proves dominant intent. If one query family remains consequentially ambiguous, refer that bounded question to `search-intent-evidence-analysis` for deeper result-and-page validation rather than duplicating its method.

Return concise query families with representative expressions, likely task and topic or entity, market and language scope, available evidence or metrics, overlap and thin-page risks, useful natural-language questions, and material uncertainties. Explain what findings could change architecture; leave audience, market and offer priority, final keyword priority, page inventory, URLs, navigation, and SEO ownership to the caller. Natural language or a small table is enough; no mandatory database or separate report.
